CA California Tax Treatment

Year-1 effective tax rate: ~0%. Taxes stay near zero for years 1–8, then rise slowly to 5–9% long-run (median). All projections in this rulebook are after federal + California tax using actual NEOS ROC percentages.

  • ROC is not currently taxable — it reduces your cost basis instead of being taxed now
  • Tiny ordinary portion (~4–14%) is absorbed by the $15k federal + $4,803 CA standard deduction in early years
  • Effective rate years 1–8: ~0%. Near-zero taxes while original-lot basis is positive
  • LTCG phases in after year 8 as original lots' basis depletes
  • Reinvestment renews basis — each year's reinvestment creates fresh lots, keeping the zero-basis fraction well below 75%; heavy discretionary spending slows this
  • Long-run effective rate: ~5–9% (median) — continuous reinvestment keeps zero-basis fraction low; heavy discretionary spending slows lot creation and can push rates to 15–20%
  • At death: step-up eliminates ALL deferred gains — the entire liability disappears for heirs

Spending Philosophy

3%/yr static raise in every healthy year. No bonus raises. Compounding bonus raises accelerate spending faster than income can sustain and increase failure risk. 3% static keeps spending predictable and leaves the surplus compounding at 14.35%.

Every year you naturally spend below target is a free improvement — the saved surplus stays in Income Engine compounding. The guardrail rules (Annual Review) handle the downside.

Discretionary Spending

How much can you take without hurting future income?

Formula:

Maximum safe discretionary = Income − Base spend − (Income Engine value × 6%)

Where:
  Income         = last 12 months of all Income Engine distributions
  Base spend     = monthly budget × 12
  Inc. Engine × 6% = reinvestment floor — twice spend growth rate, buffers NAV erosion

The 6% floor reinvests at twice the pace of spend growth, keeping the engine ahead of spending rather than merely keeping pace. Anything above the floor is genuinely surplus — for travel, purchases, gifts, etc. — without impairing the engine's future income.

Condition Action
Income is more than 50% above spend target Reset spend to 100% of spend target — income is very strong, restore full budget
Income is more than 30% above current spend Step up spend 15–25% toward spend target — income comfortably ahead, take a meaningful raise

Income is below current spend Hold spend flat — same dollar amount, no raise. Reserve covers the shortfall automatically.

Crisis cut (rare): Portfolio NAV drops do NOT trigger spending cuts. Only a genuine income crisis triggers a reduction: Reserve < 3 months of spend AND income < 70% of spend. In that case: new spend = max(current × 0.85, target × 0.80). After one reduction the floor (target × 0.80) prevents further cuts.

Historical worst case (dot-com 2000–2002, Nasdaq −78%): No spending cuts were needed. Reserve bridged income shortfalls for 5 years. Portfolio survived.

Bear Market Rules

Core principle: Never sell anything in a bear market if you can avoid it. Income Engine distributions still flow. Reserve covers shortfalls. Growth is last resort. Bear mode ends when S&P is above its 200-day MA for 3 consecutive months.
Note: Bear mode monitoring is ongoing (not scheduled). The flags tell you whether to skip Growth checks in January. Monthly Reserve bridging is always automatic regardless of bear mode.
⚠ Yellow · −15%
Stop annual checks (3–5). Keep reinvesting Income Engine surplus into Income Engine normally. Income Engine income likely still covers spending. Reserve untouched. Consider spending below your target this year.
🟠 Orange · −25%
Voluntary 15% discretionary cut recommended. If Income Engine income falls below spending → draw the shortfall only from Reserve. Stop buying anything.
🔴 Red · −40%
Hard floor: Scenario A $42,000/yr · Scenario B $70,000/yr. Draw monthly shortfall from Reserve. Do NOT sell Income Engine or Growth. Spend Income Engine income first, Reserve fills the gap. Wait for recovery (S&P above 200-day MA for 3 consecutive months).
☠ Last Resort · Reserve Gone
Sell Growth before Income Engine. Every $10k sold from QQQI removes ~$1,411/yr of income permanently. Order: VOO → QQQM → VXUS → GOOGL → SMH → SPYI → QQQI → BTCI (most volatile, sell last). If you reach SPYI, seek financial advice.

Tax Strategy & Risks

Account Placement

  • All Reserve, Income Engine, Growth assets → taxable Fidelity accounts
  • Growth must be taxable — harvest trigger requires access before age 59.5
  • Roth IRA withdrawals before 59.5 = 10% penalty on earnings
  • SPYI/QQQI: Section 1256 → 60% LTCG / 40% STCG (far better than ordinary income)
  • ~86–96% of distributions are ROC — deferred tax, effectively tax-free until cost basis depletes (~8 years per lot)
  • Step-up in basis at death = potentially $0 capital gains for heirs

Roth IRA (Separate — Not Part of Strategy)

  • Contribute $7,000/yr ($14,000 married)
  • Priority: SMH (yr 1) → GOOGL (yr 2) → QQQM (yr 3+)
  • Never harvest, never touch until age 60
  • Never include in health ratio calculation
  • Bonus tax-free wealth building alongside the strategy
  • At 60: becomes a tax-free bonus pool

Known Risks

Short Track Record QQQI launched September 2023, BTCI late 2024. All yield history is from an elevated-VIX post-COVID environment. A sustained low-VIX decade (like 2012–2019) would compress QQQI/SPYI yields to 8–10%. Monitor annually — if QQQI drops below 10% or SPYI below 8%, recalculate. Contingency (Section 1256 alternatives): QQQI → QDTE · SPYI → XDTE · BTCI → reduce to 0%, hold cash in Reserve · IAUI → YGLD. Secondary: QYLD/XYLD (Global X) — also Section 1256, but historically steeper NAV erosion. Do NOT use JEPI/JEPQ: ELN-based, 100% ordinary income, no ROC, no Section 1256 — eliminates California tax advantage immediately.
⚠ Contingency Fund Tax Warning Contingency funds use QDTE/XDTE (Roundhill, Section 1256 index options) — tax treatment is preserved. Do NOT substitute JEPI or JEPQ: they use equity-linked notes (ELNs) with 100% ordinary income and no ROC component. Switching to ELN funds eliminates the entire California tax advantage immediately. Any contingency switch is a permanent tax regime change — update estimated quarterly payments that same quarter.
⚠ NEOS Manager Concentration All four Income Engine funds (QQQI, SPYI, BTCI, IAUI) come from a single boutique provider. A regulatory change, strategy shift, fund closure, or IRS ruling against their ROC-generation method would affect the entire income engine simultaneously. Review NEOS fund AUM and operating status each January. If any fund closes, activate its contingency immediately — do not wait for the annual review. Specific "loss of faith" triggers: IRS ruling against synthetic ROC generation via Section 1256 index options; NEOS portfolio management team departure; AUM drops below $500M on any core fund (liquidity risk).
BTCI Volatility Bitcoin can drop 50–70% in a bear market. At 10% of Income Engine, a 70% BTC crash reduces Income Engine value by ~4.5%.
